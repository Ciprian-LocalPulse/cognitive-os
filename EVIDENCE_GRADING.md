# A–G editorial evidence grading
This is a transparent project rubric, **not a formal GRADE assessment** or a medical recommendation scale.

| Grade | Meaning | Review expectation |
|---|---|---|
| A | Strong | Consistent directly relevant human evidence with low major bias and adequate precision |
| B | Moderate | Relevant human support with material limitations |
| C | Promising | Preliminary human signal needing confirmation |
| D | Mixed | Meaningful unresolved disagreement across relevant findings |
| E | Limited | Sparse, weak or seriously constrained evidence |
| F | Preclinical / indirect | Mechanisms or populations/outcomes not establishing the proposed human benefit |
| G | Speculative / insufficient | No adequate verified basis for the proposed claim |

Mixed evidence is a pattern, not merely a lower study count. Unreviewed content has no assigned grade. Every grade must include confidence, population, outcome, source links, reasons, limitations and a review date. High/moderate/low/very-low confidence reflects certainty in the bounded conclusion, not expected personal benefit.

Bias, precision, consistency, applicability and publication bias matter more than counts. Counts refer only to included sources in a recorded search; overlapping reviews cannot be summed as independent trials. Unknown counts are never fabricated.

Methodological context: [GRADE Working Group](https://www.gradeworkinggroup.org/). Its certainty domains inform appraisal questions; this project's shorthand must not be presented as use of formal GRADE.
