# Safety and Ethical Boundaries

## Educational scope

Cognitive OS is an educational and informational framework. It does not diagnose, treat or cure medical or psychiatric conditions. It is not clinically validated medical software, a substitute for professional care or an independently peer-reviewed intervention. These boundaries apply to the diagrams, evidence categories, examples and software architecture, not merely to the final disclaimer.

The project gives no medication instructions, treatment protocols or personalized healthcare decisions. An evidence category is not permission to act on a health question, and a dashboard trend cannot establish a disorder. Individualized medical concerns require appropriately qualified healthcare professionals. Public discussion should not attempt to provide clinical advice to another participant.

## Uncertainty and risk communication

Evidence communication should distinguish what was measured from what is inferred. Study populations, outcomes and limitations matter even when a citation is correct. Promising findings need confirmation; mixed findings should not be summarized as uniformly favorable. Mechanistic or preclinical work remains indirect for many everyday questions.

Risk communication should identify the relevant boundary without implying that a modest-looking activity is suitable for everyone. An educational example can conflict with accessibility needs, caregiving, work requirements or an individual health restriction. The framework does not assume that those constraints can be overcome by motivation or better tracking.

## Voluntary participation

Implementation and observation should remain voluntary. A person should be able to adapt, shorten or stop an activity. Missing records should not be treated as failure, and subjective values should not become a ranking of personal worth. Coercive optimization would turn reflection into compliance and undermine the intended educational role.

The framework should not be used for workplace surveillance, employment screening, diagnosis or decisions about another person's care. Personal logs describe selected self-reports in context. They do not provide a validated measure for judging ability, reliability or health. Sharing such records requires the person's informed choice, and public issues must not expose them.

## Ordinary activity and practical limits

Protected work intervals must preserve necessary communication channels and fit real responsibilities. Learning practice should not become a punitive memory test. Sleep or movement discussion should not become an improvised treatment plan. Rest, nourishment and accessibility must not be sacrificed to reach a dashboard target.

Personal comparisons are descriptive. A favorable baseline/follow-up difference does not justify escalating an activity or replacing appropriate professional advice. An unfavorable difference does not prove personal inadequacy. Context, measurement and missingness need review before any interpretation, and a practical decision can remain modest even when uncertainty persists.

## Data ethics

Local-first design reduces the need for routine server transmission, but records and JSON exports are unencrypted. Shared profiles, backups and screenshots can expose patterns. Keep only information relevant to the question and avoid sensitive medical details. Use synthetic examples for technical discussion rather than another person's logs.

Future research involving people would require a defined protocol, informed consent, data governance and appropriate ethical oversight. Public documentation is not itself authorization to recruit participants or reuse personal records. Proposed collaborations should agree on these responsibilities before collection begins.

## Review and accountability

Contributors should identify unsupported claims, inaccessible explanations and ambiguous boundaries. Technical checks supplement this review but cannot certify safety, privacy or scientific validity. Corrections should be traceable to the affected wording and version. The author is Ciprian Ștefan Pleșca, Independent Creator and Researcher; no institutional accreditation is claimed.

The Security and Contribution policies provide reporting routes. Keeping uncertainty visible is a substantive design obligation, because a framework can mislead through presentation even when every individual citation is genuine.
