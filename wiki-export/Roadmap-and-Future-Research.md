# Roadmap and Future Research

## A question-led roadmap

The roadmap records possible development and research directions. It is not a schedule of promised features, a statement of clinical validation or a guarantee of collaboration. The current documentation edition establishes a public reading structure, conceptual architecture, evidence vocabulary and selected literature anchors. It does not establish the effectiveness of Cognitive OS as an integrated intervention.

Future work should begin with a defined question and an acceptance criterion. Adding a domain is worthwhile only when its evidence and safety boundaries can be explained. Expanding an interface is worthwhile only when the behavior, accessibility and data flow can be tested. More content or more metrics are not substitutes for clarity.

## Scientific coverage and review

Broader coverage could include additional populations, settings and outcome measures. Each extension would need source verification and contextual appraisal. Full-text review and explicit study-level risk-of-bias assessment could strengthen interpretations beyond selected metadata and abstracts. A transparent search protocol could clarify the limits of coverage without claiming exhaustiveness prematurely.

Independent academic review is a possible direction, not a completed credential. Such review should identify its scope, reviewers' roles and the claims examined. Criticism may lead to narrower statements, exclusions or revised categories rather than additional recommendations. A framework benefits from documenting those changes instead of presenting every revision as increasing certainty.

## Accessibility and usability

Manual assistive-technology review and testing with representative readers could reveal navigation or interpretation problems that automated checks miss. Clearer language, meaningful figure alternatives and predictable page structure are relevant to an academic documentation hub. A successful lint run does not show that a reader understands the distinction between category and confidence.

If executable components are evaluated, physical devices, browser behavior and keyboard workflows would need explicit coverage. Observed usability problems should be linked to a version and a reproducible scenario. Proposed fixes should be verified rather than inferred from a more attractive interface.

## Privacy and data portability

Possible improvements include stronger optional protection of exported observations, clearer recovery workflows and better separation of complete backups from tabular exports. Encryption would introduce key management and recovery questions; it should not be described as implemented before those questions are resolved. Networking or synchronization would require a new data-flow description and informed user choice.

Portability concerns both file formats and understandable meaning. A technically valid CSV is less useful if units, missingness and rating definitions are absent. Long-term interpretation also requires knowing which interface version produced the record. Future work should preserve those semantics rather than merely add export buttons.

## Educational modules and longitudinal questions

Additional modules could address new bounded questions when suitable evidence exists. They should distinguish original educational design from measured research effects. Longitudinal self-observation may raise questions about feasibility, measurement drift and patterned missingness. Informal logs alone do not constitute controlled evidence or authorize research involving participants.

A research collaboration would require an agreed protocol, responsibilities, consent, data governance and appropriate ethical oversight. Potential collaborators should be able to inspect the public Whitepaper, methodology and architecture before proposing work. No affiliations or partnerships are implied by listing these directions.

## Maintenance and acceptance

Documentation maintenance includes responding to source corrections, checking links, reviewing category definitions and preserving accurate author attribution. The public manifest and Wiki export support traceable changes. A documentation release should pass its own checks without claiming a scientific milestone that has not occurred.

The project's direction remains evidence transparency, modest implementation and descriptive observation. Roadmap changes should explain which question became clearer and which uncertainty remains. This keeps future research discussable without turning an independent documentation initiative into a collection of unsupported promises.
